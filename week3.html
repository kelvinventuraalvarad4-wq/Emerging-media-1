Something that caught my eye was when Walter Benjamin talked about the concept of “aura” in relation to art, photography, film, etc. Even though he says that reproducing an original work of art, the replica piece will lose some of its value, originality, and “the quality of its presence is always depreciated,” I agree with this to a certain point because without mechanical reproduction, only a limited on people who would know or have access to certain artwork. For example, the Mona Lisa by Leonardo da Vinci is one of the most famous paintings in the world, but most people discover it by seeing a reproduction or replica because not everyone has the opportunity to see it at the Louvre in France. In a sense, the reproduction loses the “aura,” authenticity, and historical significance of the original, but at the same time, it makes the artwork much more accessible. Benjamin wrote, “Mechanical reproduction of art changes the reaction of the masses toward art.” People can appreciate art even if they are seeing it through reproduction. Because of that, people have the opportunity to appreciate the artwork that they may have never been able to see in person, unlike in previous times when artworks were limited because it was more accessible to the wealthy and nobility. Now, original and reproduced artwork can co-exist making it easier for people to appreciate it. After reading “The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction” by Walter Benjamin, it influenced my own creativity because now I understand that it is better to use your own artwork or ideas. Using your own creativity, it could have a greater impact than recreating or re-filming what someone else has already done. For example, I would like to film a documentary about my journey from El Salvador to the United States when I was 12 years old. But there are so many similar stories, I would have to find a way to make my stories unique and accessible for it to have a stronger impact. Benjamin's perspective about originality and mechanically reproducing made me reflect that I found a voice to my documentary, its surrounding, to own its meaning and aura.
