It was interesting because futurists started this new “serate” as a new form of exhibition display which was unorthodox at the time because art was limited to two- and three- dimensional work. Despite that, Futurists held performances in theater and outdoors, which called the attention of the press in a good way. Through this new way of art, they wanted to promote nationalism and patriotism and place the audience at the center of the performance. They explored performance, theater, and art as ways to represent the impact of war on society and bring political ideas into public spaces. The historic avant-garde movements challenged traditional forms of art by allowing the public to participate, even though that was unorthodox at the time. Futurists used performance to show Italy as it was entering the modern world through war, technology, destruction, and prepare the audience to participate in his new age. Their performances became political and allowed audiences to participate. However, this led to confrontation due to different political perspectives. As the reading explains, futurism was not necessarily about empowering the audience but about redirecting its energy and attention. It was interesting to learn that futurists supported fascism. Another interesting aspect was the contrast between Russian revolutionary art and the Dada movement. They sought to involve the public and use public spaces for different purposes. Russian mass spectacles often promoted political ideology, while Dada performances challenged ideology through anarchism and opposition to established ideas. The contrast between Bogdanov’s view of art as a tool for mobilizing political sentiment and Trotsky’s support for creative freedom also demonstrated different understandings of art’s social purpose. Artistic performances communicate political messages and create a sense of collective identity. As the reading explains, “performance became the privileged paradigm for artistic and political operations in the public sphere.”
