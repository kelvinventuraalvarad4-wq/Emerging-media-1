This reading was interesting because it explored performance, theater, and art as ways to represent the impact of war on society and bring political ideas into public spaces. The historic avant-garde movements challenged traditional forms of art by allowing the public to participate, even though that was unorthodox at the time. Futurists used performance to show Italy as entering the modern world through war, technology, and destruction. Their performances became political and allowed audiences to participate. However, this led to confrontation due to different political perspectives. As the reading explains, futurism was not necessarily about empowering the audience but about redirecting its energy and attention. It was interesting to learn that futurists supported fascism. Another interesting aspect was the contrast between Russian revolutionary art and the Dada movement. They sought to involve the public and use public spaces for different purposes. Russian mass spectacles often promoted political ideology, while Dada performances challenged ideology through anarchism and opposition to established ideas. The contrast between Bogdanov’s view of art as a tool for mobilizing political sentiment and Trotsky’s support for creative freedom also demonstrated different understandings of art’s social purpose. Artistic performances communicate political messages and create a sense of collective identity. As the reading explains, “performance became the privileged paradigm for artistic and political operations in the public sphere.”
